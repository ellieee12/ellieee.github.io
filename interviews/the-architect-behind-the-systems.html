The Architect Behind the Systems

I had the opportunity to interview Boris Billy, an Information Systems Architect at Akkodis, an IT services company with over 10 years of experience in the field. He explained that his role as an IS architect involves designing applications, assisting with the budget management of systems and software, organizing and mapping applications and their interactions, as well as keeping up with technological trends to best meet business needs.

Boris also clarified that there are different levels of architecture, starting from technical architecture, software architecture, and finally, IS architecture. A technical architect focuses on the design within a specific technical stack, a software architect conceptualizes entire software systems, and an IS architect manages all software across a company, spanning multiple domains.

Before the interview, I was drawn to the idea of becoming an architect because I enjoyed modeling and conceptualizing problems into feasible solutions. However, Boris highlighted that the role also requires expertise in networking, cybersecurity, work methodologies, various frameworks, AI implementation, and even commercial knowledge such as risk evaluation, budgeting, and awareness of software development costs. His favorite part of the role is finding technical solutions that meet client needs while respecting given constraints.

I also asked him about the current job market and the potential for AI to replace architects. He explained that while AI can assist in generating pseudocode or proof-of-concept designs, it cannot replace architects because it cannot fully account for every aspect of a software system or accurately evaluate potential problems and risks.

Regarding the job market, Boris noted a high demand for professionals capable of design and conception over pure development. However, he emphasized that fresh graduates cannot immediately step into an architect role; typically, it takes at least five years to become a technical architect, and further experience is needed to progress to software and IS architect positions, as one must understand the full range of technologies and processes involved. He also mentioned that certain roles, such as project manager, are becoming less in demand as teams increasingly manage themselves autonomously.

When I asked for advice for someone pursuing a career as an architect, he encouraged having an open mind, avoiding tunnel vision on a single technology, and continually reassessing whether practices from five years ago still apply today.
