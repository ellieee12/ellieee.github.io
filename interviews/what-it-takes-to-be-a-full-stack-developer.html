What It Takes to Be a Full-Stack Developer

In order to learn more about the job market of being a developer in Malaysia, I contacted Mohamad Suhairi MD Subhi, an alumni of ENSEEIHT who is now working as a full-stack developer at Telekom Malaysia, one of the largest government-linked communication companies in the country.

One of the challenges faced by Suhairi as a full-stack developer is keeping up with constantly emerging new technologies and the dilemma of choosing which ones to use for a project. Even though the responsibility of selecting the appropriate framework lies on the shoulder of the solution architect, it is also important for him as a full-stack developer to be able to catch up and properly implement the new technologies.

As there has been increasing discussions around the possibility of artificial intelligence replacing the jobs of software developers, I decided to also ask him regarding his opinion on this issue. Suhairi expressed that he also uses artificial intelligence when coding but to say that it will replace real engineers is a bit of a stretch as software development is not only the code we produce, but also the security, performance, integration and reliability of the software itself. However, he also added that perhaps in the near future, the job market will shift from needing developers who code, to developers who fix and maintain software that is coded by artificial intelligence.

I also asked him about his work environment at Telekom Malaysia. The reason as to why I had this inquiry in mind is the fact that there is a very real possibility that I will be working with them after my graduation at ENSEEIHT, since Telekom Malaysia was the one who granted me my scholarship of excellency to pursue my studies in France. When asked about this, Suhairi explained that the overall working environment is alright but for now, there is not enough manpower and he is unable to do work from home.

At the end of the interview, he encouraged me to keep up with new technology, be curious and have fun along the way. As someone who genuinely enjoys solving challenging problems and learning about new things, his advice definitely served as a reminder of why I chose to become an engineer in the first place.
