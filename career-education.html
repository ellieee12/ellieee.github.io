Learning Log

Overcoming Challenges In a New Industry

During my internship at Komax France as a software developer, I was introduced to the world of electrical harness manufacturing while developing a module to import standard aviation data into their manufacturing machines. As a computer science student with little prior knowledge of electrical harnesses, the task was both fascinating and intimidating.

My responsibility was to import data containing all the components of an electrical harness and transform it into a format readable by Komax's machines, which provide step-by-step instructions for technicians. This required understanding the components and some manufacturing techniques. To bridge my knowledge gap, I conducted my own research and sought guidance from experienced colleagues. As an introvert, it was initially challenging to ask questions, but over time I became more confident and learned a great deal.

Through this experience, I not only developed software modules that integrate with existing systems but also gained valuable industrial knowledge about electrical harness manufacturing. Collaborating with colleagues helped me improve my communication skills and overcome shyness. Ultimately, the module I developed contributed to a more effective demonstration of Komax's products during the Zuken Innovation World 2025 showroom in Paris.

These experiences taught me that adaptability is not just a personal strength but also a valuable asset in computer science, where technologies, tools, and methodologies evolve constantly. Learning new frameworks, understanding unfamiliar problem domains, and integrating into diverse teams all require the same resilience and willingness to step outside my comfort zone. In the end, I've learned to adapt, learn fast, and thrive in new environments, skills that continue to shape me as both a developer and an individual.

Overcoming Challenges In a New Industry

Just like a water bear, an exceptionally adaptable micro-animal known for surviving extreme environments, I have learned to adapt to unfamiliar situations and acquire new skills quickly. Before arriving in France in 2022, I did not speak French, yet I had only one year to master the language. Starting my Bachelor's degree in Toulouse was challenging, as all courses were in French and it was difficult to make friends at first. I quickly realized that the most effective way to learn anything is through consistent practice, so I pushed myself to speak, ask questions, and interact with classmates, even with my broken accent and imperfect grammar.

During my internship, I had to adapt once again, this time by using my fourth language in a professional setting, learning how to communicate with colleagues, and navigating workplace expectations. Through perseverance, I succeeded in integrating into my team, completed my project, and was later accepted into an engineering school in France. Today, I follow all my classes with ease and have built friendships with locals, broadening my worldview.
